# Decisions for review

Every choice the build has left to a person, in one place. The project lead's rule of Nov 21 is
that nothing is asked of a person mid-build: wherever a choice needs one, the default is built,
marked `PLAN-OPEN` in the code where the PRD leaves it open, and listed here with who decides, the
default built, a recommendation, what a later change would cost and where it lives. This version
describes the code at commit `c307a0b`, with phases 0 to 4 built and closed; phases 5 to 10 and 12
are designed (phase 11 is people's work, on devices). Each design's own defaults join this document
as its phase closes, except phase 5's, which are listed already from its reviewed design, ahead of
its build, so that each marker the build writes can name its row. Read "Decide early" first, then
your own section, and answer by row id: a row nobody answers keeps its default.
It holds 159 decisions: 26 for the project lead, 84 for Brooke and Mercy, 13 for counsel and 36
for the team; a row that needs someone else as well names them in brackets after its question.
"Before the first real patient" lists what needs a person or an account but no decision.

## Decide early

Most rows below are a constant, a sentence or a new version of a form, and cost little to change
at any time. These few do not: they cut across the data model, the API's permission checks, the
phone and the portal, or decide what reaches EMS, billing or an export, so a late answer costs a
rebuild of several parts, or cannot undo what was already sent.

1. **Who may see, co-sign, attest or take over a note with a GU, breast or rectal exam (BM-4).**
   Until it is answered, such notes wait for attestation on any team whose attendings lack
   `patients.view_sensitive_exams`. The rule is one function on the server, mirrored on the phone
   and in the phone's test server; a later change alters who reads every such note already
   charted.
2. **Whether a Non-Medical note inside a Medical visit is signed (BM-5).** A late change touches
   the sign rules on the server, the phone and the test server, and every note completed before it
   stays unsigned, so phase 10's billing export would read two kinds of note for good.
3. **When an offline signature counts, and under which credential (CO-3).** Both times and both
   credentials are stored, so no data is lost; but the billing export reads the signature time,
   and claims sent under one answer stay sent.
4. **42 CFR Part 2 and the sensitive code list (CO-1, CO-2).** Who reads a treatment record or a
   sensitive diagnosis is decided in the API's read rule, its copy on the phone, the EMS handoff
   and phase 10's exports. What was read or sent under the old answer can't be called back.
5. **What reaches EMS (BM-18, TM-3).** The handoff's lines are built by both interpreters and
   pinned by their vectors and the golden file, and a PDF handed to a medic is out of the team's
   hands.
6. **The forms' clinical choices, before the first real chart (BM-28, BM-30, BM-31, BM-33 to
   BM-40, BM-43, BM-46 to BM-56, TM-13).** A released definition never changes: a change is a new
   version, every note keeps the version it was written on, and everything that reads notes (the
   read model, and phase 10's billing export and grant reports) reads every version for good. A
   frozen list (the body regions, the Social needs icons) becomes a new list under a new name. The
   other forms rows cost a change to the phone's widgets or renderer, to the interpreters, or a new
   note type beside the others, and change nothing already stored.
7. **The ID link host, and the app's public name, bundle identifiers and domain (TM-1, TM-2).** A
   printed card never expires and names its host for good, so a new host means reprinting every
   card or keeping the old one forwarding; bundle identifiers can't change once an app is in the
   stores.
8. **Retention of photos, documents and the audit log (CO-10).** Phase 12 anchors the audit chain
   under compliance-mode Object Lock, which cannot be shortened once set.
9. **Whether the field specification's lines stay in the repository (PL-5).** A late no means
   taking `packages/forms/definitions/spec/*.txt` out of the repository, and out of its history if
   they must not stay there at all, which every clone and the PR branch must then follow; and,
   since the spec check reads those files, reading every definition against the tab by hand from
   then on.
10. **The problem code system (BM-21).** Every stored problem carries an ICD-10-CM code, which is
    what billing exports; another system later means mapping every stored problem, and claims
    already sent stay coded as they were.
11. **What a preset may never change once made (BM-64, BM-73, PL-20).** An item's unit, a note
    type's protected parts and who may read its sections, and a flag's sensitivity are fixed, so
    that counts and hidden history keep their meaning. Loosening one later means every past order
    and count reading its unit from its version, or history hidden from some readers becoming
    theirs to read, which can't be called back once read.

## How this document is kept

- Each phase's close adds its rows: every default and question in its design that needs a person,
  in its decider's section, under the next free id. Ids are never reused or renumbered. Phase 5's
  rows come from its design, ahead of its build (PL-12 to PL-26, BM-63 to BM-84, CO-13, TM-36), so
  its close checks each against what was built and adds the files its markers are in.
- A row is a question only a person can answer. A decision the project lead has already taken in a
  document is not listed, unless the document says it is still open.
- A `PLAN-OPEN:` marker in code names its row, for example
  `PLAN-OPEN (TM-6): how long an untouched draft stays on its author's phone`. Several markers
  about one question are one row, naming all their files. Most markers at `c307a0b` name their
  design item instead (lifecycle 12.1 #4, design 14.1 #2), and the rest only their question. Each
  row's Where names every file that holds one of its markers, and every marker belongs to exactly
  one row. `node tools/decisions-check.mjs` checks this, in CI and in `tools/ci-local.sh`: a marker
  that names its row must name one that exists and whose Where names the marker's file; an older
  marker without a row id is matched to its row by `tools/decisions-check-rules.mjs`, which holds
  its opening words and only shrinks; and a marker with neither fails the check.
- A row whose Where says "no marker" has none in code, for one of three reasons: nothing is built
  for it yet (a later phase's default, or a question with no default); it is a policy or a way of
  working, not code; or its default sits in a released definition or its piece's files, which the
  forms lock keeps unchanged (`packages/forms/definitions.lock.json`), so no marker can be added
  there.
- When a person decides, the decision goes into the plan's D-table with the date and who took it,
  the row leaves this document (its id stays retired), and its markers go or say what was decided.
  The plan's section 6 keeps the reasoning for each default until then.
